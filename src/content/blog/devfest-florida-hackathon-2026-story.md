### Should We Cancel DevFest?

A few weeks before DevFest Florida Hackathon 2026, our organizing team found ourselves asking a question I honestly didn't expect, though I wasn't surprised to hear it, largely due to some heavy work pressures I was dealing with at the time.

> **"Should we cancel the event?"**

Man, that's not an easy conversation to have when you've already invested months of planning, coordinating speakers, reaching out to sponsors, and preparing something for the developer community.

It was early October, and after receiving some concerns about registration numbers, we started wondering whether we could reach our goal of 100 participants before November 6th.

And believe me, when you're helping organize a community event, that uncertainty can get a little uncomfortable. 😄

But here's something I've learned over the years:

> **Sometimes, when things aren't going as planned, you need to look at the data, trust your team, and remember why you started this in the first place.**

And that's exactly what happened.

Fast-forward to November 6&ndash;8, 2026, at <a href="https://www.ucf.edu/downtown/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">UCF Downtown</a> in Orlando.

We had **127 ticket registrations**, **33 projects** in our <a href="https://devfest-hackathon-2026.devpost.com/project-gallery" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Devpost gallery</a>, **19 volunteers**, and an incredible group of speakers, mentors, sponsors, and community members.

Not bad for an event we briefly considered canceling, right? 😄

But the numbers are only part of the story.

¡Hola! 👋 I'm Javi, a Software Engineer and <a href="https://gdg.community.dev/u/javier4/#/about" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">GDG Central Florida Organizer</a>. I've spent years helping organize developer events because I believe sharing knowledge and creating opportunities can make a difference in someone's journey.

This is a little behind-the-scenes story of what we experienced while organizing <a href="https://devfestflorida.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><strong>DevFest Florida Hackathon 2026</strong></a>, the challenges we faced, and what our community reminded me about teamwork.

---

### A Little Data Changed Our Perspective

During one of our organizing discussion meetings, <a href="https://gil.dev/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Gil Creque</a> brought something interesting to the table.

**Historical registration data.**

Gil had examined previous DevFest Florida events and noticed something worth considering: registrations often increased significantly as the event approached.

Apparently, developers and tech-curious people like to wait until the last minute to register. Who would've thought? 😄

Our GDG Central Florida founder, <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/in/artinmotionllc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Christi Kapp</a>, also reminded us of this pattern based on her experience organizing these events.

That information helped us look at the situation differently.

Instead of making a decision based only on how registrations looked several weeks before the event, we started considering what our historical trends were telling us.

Now, historical data doesn't guarantee future results.

But it gave us something we needed at that moment.

**Perspective and Hope.**

We had an opportunity to change our approach, increase our efforts, and give our community another reason to get excited about the event.

So rather than canceling, we decided to push forward.

And that's where the teamwork really started to show.

---

### Getting the Word Out, One Post at a Time

One thing I've learned from organizing developer events is that having a great program doesn't automatically mean people will hear about it.

**You still have to communicate its value.**

And sometimes you need to get a little creative.

I personally started experimenting with short-form videos, promotional images, social media posts, and even some fun <a href="https://photos.app.goo.gl/Ahjcb5yYR9AhY3A18" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">GTA-inspired promos</a>, given all the buzz around Grand Theft Auto releasing this November, along with some good old-fashioned community outreach.

Those who know me are probably not surprised that I created a few infographics along the way. 😄

I reached out to several of our <a href="https://devfestflorida.com/sponsors" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">sponsors</a> and encouraged them to help spread the word through their networks.

As our <a href="https://devfestflorida.com/speakers" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">speakers</a> were announced, we also had the opportunity to share their sessions and workshops with other developer communities. I also asked the speakers to help us promote our hackathon.

And our organizing team worked to connect with groups throughout Central Florida.

A special thank-you to <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/in/augustincaitlin/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Caitlin</a> and the <a href="https://www.orlandodevs.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Orlando Devs</a> community for helping share our event. Communities supporting other communities is something I always love to see.

Orlando Devs (ODevs) provides a connection point for several developer groups throughout the Orlando area, and that kind of collaboration matters.

We also received great support from Gil Creque and <a href="https://space-coast.dev/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Space Coast Devs</a>, a developer community just east of Orlando along the Space Coast, whose help spreading the word made a real difference.

We weren't simply asking people to register for another event.

We wanted to show them what they could experience:

* **Learn** from developers and technical leaders.
* **Explore** technologies like AI, Cloud, Firebase, Unity, XR, and other technologies.
* **Meet** others with similar interests.
* **Build** something useful with a team.
* **Leave** with new knowledge, connections, and maybe even a project worth adding to their portfolio.

For me, this became another reminder of something I've been studying lately: **technical communication**.

> **It's not enough to have something valuable to offer. People need to understand that value before they can act on it.**

And slowly, the registrations started moving.

### Then the Community Showed Up

By the time DevFest Florida Hackathon 2026 arrived, we had surpassed our original registration goal.

**127 registrations!**

And by the end of the event, the Devpost project gallery featured **33 projects**.

I have to admit, seeing those numbers after our earlier discussions felt pretty good.

But what made me happiest wasn't simply reaching our goal.

It was seeing people show up, find teammates, ask questions, learn something new, and start building.

That's what these events are supposed to be about.

We had three hackathon tracks:

* **Devs Track:** Building useful software, including web applications, APIs, mobile solutions, and machine learning projects.
* **Game Devs Track:** Creating playable prototypes, interactive experiences, and inventive game concepts.
* **Data for Good Track:** Using data and technology to explore problems that affect communities.

Each track offered participants a different way to turn an idea into something meaningful.

### A Weekend of Learning, Building, and Sharing

One of my favorite things about DevFest is bringing together people with different levels of experience.

You might have someone who has been writing software for twenty years sitting next to someone attending their first hackathon.

And both can learn something.

**One moment that really touched me was seeing fathers and sons hacking together.** Sharing ideas, learning, and building something side by side. Man, that was special to see. ❤️

It reminded me that these events aren't just about technology. They're also about creating memories, strengthening connections, and maybe inspiring the next generation.

And that's something you can't measure with registrations or analytics.

This year's event featured a wonderful group of speakers and technical sessions.

Caitlin Augustin from <a href="https://www.datakind.org/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">DataKind</a> and ODevs organizer and <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/in/nickawalsh/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Nick Walsh</a> (Principal at <a href="https://envylabs.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Envy Labs</a>) shared the keynote, helping set the stage for a weekend focused on building and creating meaningful solutions.

We also had speakers exploring a variety of topics, including:

* <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/in/loiane" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><strong>Loiane Groner</strong></a>, from BNY, discussing specs-driven development, harness engineering, and AI-assisted software development.
* <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/in/arthur-thompson-02a275258/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><strong>Arthur Thompson</strong></a>, from Google, sharing how to build AI applications using the Google ecosystem.
* <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/in/carolcorybolger" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><strong>Carol Bolger</strong></a>, leading a hands-on workshop on grounded Gemini agents using Flutter and Firebase.
* <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/in/wpgorman/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><strong>Will Gorman</strong></a>, exploring agentic engineering and how AI agents are changing software development.
* <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/in/dwane-hemmings" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><strong>Dwane Hemmings</strong></a>, from Vonage, demonstrating WebXR with real-time communications.
* <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/in/javujavichi/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><strong>Javiera Laso</strong></a>, from Thoughtworks, sharing an approach to AI-assisted architecture review boards.
* <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/in/muntasersyed/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><strong>Muntaser Syed</strong></a>, Gen AI Engineer / Architect, exploring Hashrope, a data structure implemented across multiple programming languages.

What I enjoyed about the program was the variety.

From AI agents and software architecture to extended reality and data structures, there was something for developers at different stages of their learning journey.

And as someone who enjoys public speaking and sharing technical knowledge, I appreciate how much work goes into preparing a presentation or workshop.

To every speaker who shared their time, knowledge, and experience with our community:

**Thank you!** 🙏

You gave our attendees something valuable to take home.

### What This Experience Taught Me

I've been involved with Google Developer Groups for several years now.

And even after organizing meetups, conferences, and community activities, I still find myself learning something new.

This event reminded me of a few important lessons:

1. **First, don't make decisions based on fear alone.**  
   When our registration numbers raised concerns, we could have decided to cancel. But examining historical information helped us understand the situation more clearly.
2. **Second, communication matters.**  
   Creating promotional material, explaining the value of the event, reaching out to sponsors, and connecting with other communities all required communication. And those are skills I've been deliberately working on improving in my own professional journey.
3. **Third, community is about helping others succeed.**  
   One of the most rewarding parts of organizing events is seeing someone walk away with new knowledge, a new connection, or a little more confidence to take the next step.

Maybe they learned a technology they had never used.

Maybe they met someone who could help them with their next project.

Or maybe they discovered that they're more capable than they originally thought.

Those are the moments I care about.

> **Success isn't always about how many people show up. It's also about what people take with them when they leave.**

And finally, I was reminded that being part of a great team means trusting the strengths and experiences of others.

No one person has all the answers.

But when people come together with a shared purpose, they can accomplish something pretty special.
